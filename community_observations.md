## Selected Reading 1: AI Data Centers

Title: Paying for AI's Data Centers Would Take 250 Million Jobs.
America Has 159 Million

Source:
https://postquantum.com/leadership/paying-for-ai-data-centers/

### Description

This article discusses the financial challenges of building and
operating AI data centers. The author examines the large investments
being made in AI infrastructure and questions whether companies
will generate enough revenue to recover those costs.

The article uses financial estimates, calculations, and comparisons
with employment costs to explain the argument.

### Observation

I noticed that technology discussions can involve detailed
explanations supported by numbers and calculations. The author
does not simply express an opinion but explains the assumptions
behind the financial estimates.

This reading helped me identify evidence-based arguments as
one possible distinction for my classification model.

## Selected Reading 3: Solar Panels on a Swiss Dam

Title: Engineers Hung 4,872 Solar Panels on a Swiss Dam
8,200 Feet Above Sea Level

Source:
https://www.gadgetreview.com/engineers-hung-4872-solar-panels-on-a-swiss-dam-8200-feet-above-sea-level-it-outperforms-lowland-plants-3-8x-in-winter

### Description

This article discusses a renewable energy project in Switzerland
where engineers installed 4,872 solar panels on a dam.

The article explains how the location, temperature, and panel
angles affect electricity production. It also reports that
the installation produced more electricity during winter
than a reference solar installation at a lower elevation.

### Observation

I noticed that technology news can present achievements that
may lead readers to express surprise, excitement, or concern.

For example, readers might react to the engineering challenges
or the reported electricity production without providing a
detailed technical explanation.

This reading helped me consider emotional reactions as a
third possible distinction for my classification model.

## Three Candidate Distinctions

### 1. Evidence-Based Arguments

Text that supports a position with facts, statistics,
calculations, examples, or explanations.

### 2. Strong Opinions

Text that expresses a clear personal judgment or position
with limited supporting explanation.

### 3. Emotional Reactions

Text that mainly expresses feelings such as excitement,
surprise, frustration, or concern.

## Additional Observations

While reviewing these articles, I noticed that technology
discussions cover many different subjects. Some focus on
technical performance or financial information, while others
raise questions about privacy, security, or social impacts.

I also noticed that the three candidate distinctions may
overlap. A person could express a strong opinion while
providing evidence or showing an emotional reaction.

This could make some examples difficult to classify.

For the next milestone, I will need to define each label
clearly and create a decision rule for examples that
could belong to more than one category.
